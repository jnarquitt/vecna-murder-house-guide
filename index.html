1 At a Glance

What is this?

A dark, survival-horror one-shot for a party of 4–6 characters, level 10. The party wakes up in body bags in a undead-infested graveyard in the Shadowfell and must fight their way into a haunted Victorian house to survive — and break a century-old curse laid by a cult of Vecna, the god of dark secrets.

Tone

Haunted house / Ravenloft-style horror. Zombies, ghosts, a murdered family, two creepy possessed dolls, and a cult ritual in the basement. Spooky and gross, not graphic — see Teen Table Notes for how to dial it in for a younger crowd.

Runtime

Written length runs 3–4 hours. This guide includes a trimmed 2–2.5 hour convention/club path — look for the TIME-SAVER tags throughout the walkthrough.

What you need

This device (no other book needed), dice, and characters. No pregens shipped with the original module — six ready-to-play level 10 pregens are included below in Pregen Characters.

The One-Sentence Pitch

Say this to hook the table You wake up zipped inside a body bag in a fog-choked graveyard full of the walking dead — your only shelter is a beautiful old house that is very obviously not what it seems.

Map of the Adventure (Flow)

Graveyard (zombie horde)→ Foyer (illusion feast)→ Ruined Foyer→ Kitchen (locked doors + grate)→ Dining Room (wraiths)→ Twins' Room (dolls)→ Bell's Room (clue)→ Master Bedroom (clue)→ Basement Ritual Chamber (final fight)

The house only has a few rooms — players can explore the upstairs rooms (Circle of Wraiths, Baby Doll Sisters, Sister Bloody Sister, Paradise Lost) in any order before heading down into the basement. Nothing is strictly locked except the two side doors in the kitchen and the basement grate/gate (passphrase: “Vecna Forever”).

Win Condition

Destroy the Pendant of Vecna Influence (dissolve in holy water for 1 hour, or otherwise remove/negate it) to free the ghosts of twins Ela and Lila and lift the curse on the house.

2 Before The Table Sits Down

15-Minute Prep Checklist

  • Skim The Curse Unleashed once so you can improvise questions about the family.
  • Print or open Pregen Characters on a second device/tab, or have players pick one if no characters are pre-made.
  • Decide your time budget and pick a stopping point in advance (see Runtime above).
  • Read the Teen Table Notes section and decide how you'll handle the succubus/kiss scene and the zombie horde count.
  • Have dice ready for players who forget theirs (a shared dice pool is fine for a one-shot).
  • Bookmark DC Cheat Sheet — every check in the module in one table.
  • Optional: queue up spooky ambient sound (fog, wind, distant chains) on a phone/speaker for atmosphere.

Running It As a Con/Store One-Shot

GM Tip This module was written as one chapter of a bigger "Vecna: Eve of Ruin" multi-table convention epic (with an Elminster framing device and a dracolich finale). You do not need any of that for a standalone table — this guide treats Vecna: Murder House purely as its own complete one-shot. If you're curious about the bigger frame it was built for, see the optional appendix note in Credits.
3 Teen Table Notes

This module is horror-flavored (undead, gore descriptions, a murdered family, a cursed pendant, and a demon in disguise). It is very runnable for teens with a few dial-adjustments. Nothing here is sexual or gratuitously graphic in the original text, but a couple of moments benefit from a lighter touch.

Content heads-up Descriptions of a family massacre (off-screen, discovered after the fact), rotting corpses, a disease-inflicting ghoul, two dead children reanimated as "creepy dolls," and a captive woman who is secretly a shapeshifting fiend (a succubus, renamed "Siren of Shadows" in this module) that tries to charm and drain a PC. Consider a quick lines-and-veils check-in before you start: "this session has zombies, some gore, and one creepy-doll jump scare — anyone want anything toned down?"
The Siren of Shadows scene The rescued "wizardess" in the Ritual Chamber is actually a succubus. Her Charm and Draining Kiss abilities are written as a seductive kiss that drains life. For a teen table, reflavor this as her simply grabbing/biting the nearest hero and draining their vitality — skip any romantic framing entirely. Mechanically nothing needs to change, just narrate it as a monster attack, not a kiss.
The zombie horde As written, waves of 100 zombies (2 waves of 50) attack the party at the start — way too many to run individually in a con timeslot. Use the Dungeon Master's Guide "mob" shortcut instead: describe an overwhelming wall of undead, but only ever put 6–10 zombie tokens in play at once, narrating the rest as a rising tide the party is racing to outrun to the house door. See Scene 1 Start Here for the trimmed version.
Player safety valve Agree on a simple pause word (e.g., "cut") any player can say to skip past a description without breaking the group's momentum. You (the GM) narrate a shorter, softer version and move on.
Keep it fun Lean into the campy horror-movie energy: the devil serving a fancy dinner, the dolls giggling "I killed my sister!", chains rattling in the basement. Playing it a little theatrical keeps it thrilling rather than upsetting for a teen crowd.
4 The Curse Unleashed (Backstory)

You don't need to read this aloud — it's for you, so you can improvise NPC answers and drop hints. The two in-fiction diaries the party can find (see Bell's Room and Master Bedroom) reveal pieces of this naturally.

The Reaper Family

The Reapers were volunteer gravekeepers for the Neverwinter Cemetery, living in a house the city gave them. Father Ellemon Reaper found a strange amulet buried near a grave — a severed left hand with a desiccated eye. It was a fragment of Vecna's power.

The Fall

Touching the amulet bound Ellemon to Vecna as a warlock. Over years, Vecna's whispers drove him to kidnap and murder visitors to the graveyard for their secrets, dissolving the bodies in the basement. The evil radiating from the house eventually dragged the entire property sideways into the Shadowfell, mirrored into the twisted city of Evernight (a dark reflection of Neverwinter).

The Massacre

One night, an unseen force tore through the house and slaughtered Ellemon, his wife, and his eldest daughter Bell in their sleep. His youngest daughters — twins Ela and Lila — had their souls corrupted and trapped inside their favorite dolls, dooming them to haunt the ruined house.

100 Years Later

A cult of Vecna rediscovered the abandoned, phased-out house and resumed dark rituals in the basement — the same ritual that has just pulled the PCs into the Shadowfell against their will.

Full Original Flavor Text

Read the full published backstory (optional flavor)

The Reaper family tradition was to tend to the graves of the Neverwinter Cemetery as volunteers doing the work of the gods of light. They lived in a lovely home donated by the city of Neverwinter after years of living in poverty.

One early evening, after a long day of digging graves, Ellemon Reaper, father of three lovely daughters, was walking among the gravestones when a dark mist on the ground caught his attention. He found an amulet half-buried in the dirt that seemed to be beckoning to him. Thinking it a lost trinket, Ellemon decided to secure it for lost and found. The amulet, however, had other plans.

Upon touching the cursed artifact, he was linked to its evil energy and forced to divulge dark secrets overheard at funerals in exchange for mystical powers — his warlock boons. It was in his middle-aged years, after the birth of his twin daughters, that the voices in his head drove him to murder. Following the commands of Vecna, his warlock patron, he began stalking and kidnapping members of high society who visited the graveyard grounds, keeping their corpses in his basement and dissolving them in dark rituals.

As the raw evil of the god of dark secrets grew, it began shifting the alignment of the undertaker's house, phasing it, along with Ellemon's family, to the Evernight Cemetery. Then, one especially evil night, an unspeakable horror befell the family — a powerful unseen force mutilated Ellemon, his wife, and his oldest daughter in their sleep. The two youngest daughters, the twins, had their souls corrupted and trapped in their favorite dolls.

The baby doll sisters continued living on in the Shadowfell for generations as the structure fell into ruin. One hundred years later, a small sect of the cult of Vecna discovered the abandoned residence and once again dark rituals were regularly performed in the basement — the same ritual that has brought the PCs here against their will.

5 Cast Of Characters

Eldon — Priest of Pelor

A scholarly cleric who wakes up alongside the party, also abducted by crimson-robed cultists. Friendly and informative — he knows they're in the Evernight Cemetery, a Shadowfell mirror of Neverwinter, and that "crevices of dusk" are portals home. He's a full ally NPC (stat block in Bestiary) — happy to fight alongside the party or hang back and heal.

Voice cue: gentle, a little nerdy, quotes scripture at odd moments, unfailingly optimistic even covered in graveyard mud.

The Feast Devil

A horned, bat-winged fiend who greets the party in the illusory foyer, inviting them to "share in the feast of Asmodeus." Pure roleplay encounter — smooth-talking, unbothered by threats, vanishes the instant it's attacked or once the party makes a choice, revealing the house's true ruined state.

Voice cue: silky, aristocratic, never raises its voice, delighted by discomfort.

Ela & Lila — The Baby Doll Sisters

Twin 8-year-old sisters, souls trapped in porcelain dolls, driven mad by the curse. One doll is blood-spattered with an X on its forehead; the other giggles "I killed my sister!" Both try to possess the nearest PC and force them to attack allies. Destroying the dolls (not the souls) turns them friendly — they become sweet, sad ghost children who beg the party to break the curse.

Voice cue: singsong, childish, unsettling calm about violence, heartbreakingly grateful once freed.

The "Wizardess" — Siren of Shadows

Found chained in the ritual circle, crying for help. The instant she's freed she attacks — she's a succubus in disguise. Destroying/defacing her magic circle banishes her outright with no fight needed, if the party is clever or you're short on time.

Voice cue (before reveal): frightened, sobbing, desperate. After reveal: no dialogue needed — straight to combat.

Cultists of Vecna

Three robed fanatics performing the ritual in the basement. One wears the Pendant of Vecna Influence. They fight to the death alongside their fiendish allies — no negotiating.

Ellemon Reaper (deceased)

Never appears in person — his story is told through diaries and the dining room corpse ("Mother? Is that you?"). His remorse (he wanted to destroy the pendant but couldn't bring himself to) is the emotional core of the house.

7 Encounter Walkthrough

#1 Start Here Graveyard

Read to first player You wake into darkness and gasp a deep breath of rotted air, as if you were under the surface of foul, stinking water for far too long. You find yourself restrained within a hempen blanket you can't get out of — a body bag! What do you do?
Read once free The night air of a cemetery greets you like a dance floor for gloomy fog. Random zombies lumber between gravestones but pay you no mind. A dark altar sits before you, just outside a gravekeeper's Victorian structure. You realize you were lying on a pile of body-bagged corpses — and one of the other bags is starting to move.
GM Notes Wake each PC in turn (creative escapes welcome — a dagger cut works fine). Eldon, a priest of Pelor, also wakes among them — he was abducted by crimson-robed cultists and remembers nothing after the ritual began (an Insight check DC 13 confirms he's telling the truth).
The Altar If searched: jars of pickled body parts, a gold candle, four silver bars (1 x 500oz, 3 x 250oz), and a potted Belladonna plant (Nature DC 15 to identify — hard to germinate). The altar bears Vecna's symbol — a severed left hand with a desiccated eye (Religion DC 15 to identify if not already known).
The Horde Zombies ignore the party unless provoked. A bloated ghoul eventually rushes the party, and the noise draws a horde — written as two waves of 50 Shadow-Touched zombies pushing the party toward the house (the high ground of the escarpment slows the dead).
Time-saver Run this as a tense retreat, not a full battle. Put 1 bloated ghoul + 6–8 zombie tokens on the field, narrate hundreds more shambling in from the fog. Let the party fight or flee toward the lit windows — either way, they end up at the front door within a few rounds.

#2 Dark Welcoming Foyer

Read on entry Inside, you find yourself in a fine foyer with polished furnishings and a lovely piano. An open door leads to a kitchen with a large table hosting an appetizing feast. Suddenly, a gargoyle-like devilish figure appears in the doorway — horns, bat wings, a serpentine tail. "Ah, so it appears we have guests that wish to share in the feast of Asmodeus."
GM Notes The horned devil is purely a roleplay/illusion encounter. Whatever the party does — dine, chat, refuse, attack — it vanishes the instant it's attacked, or once a decision is made. Then the illusion collapses.
Read after the reveal The beautiful Victorian décor changes as the devilish figure vanishes in black shadow and smoke. A flash of black light later, you're somewhere else, yet somehow the same place — a ruined version of the house. Flies swarm a pile of stinking corpses before a demolished piano. Fine furnishings are rubble. The kitchen door is rotted, sagging shut.
Eldon's Info If Eldon is present, he explains they're in the Evernight Cemetery, a Shadowfell mirror of Neverwinter — a city of the dead. He suggests seeking a "crevice of dusk," a portal home. (Only relevant for continuing the wider campaign — skip if running as a pure one-shot.)

#3 Remnants of Yesterday's Feast Kitchen

Read on entry The kitchen is completely ruined. Shattered furnishings lie in rotting splinters; a cold hearth rests beside a stove buried in rubble. Two doors sit off to the left, both closed.
GM Notes Both side doors are locked — DC 18 Sleight of Hand (Thieves' Tools) or Athletics to force. Nothing of value inside them.
The Grate A DC 18 Perception or Investigation check finds a padlocked grate under rubble in the corner — the way to the basement (Scene #8). The magic padlock has no keyhole; it opens on the passphrase "Vecna Forever" (also breakable with Dispel Magic DC 22). It otherwise acts as an invisible wall of force.
The Prisoner Cry On a later pass through the kitchen (after clearing the upstairs), rattling chains and a maiden's cries for help can be heard from the grate (passive Perception 14+ or DC 14 Perception/Investigation). This is bait leading to the Ritual Chamber.

#4 Circle of Wraiths Dining Room

Read on entry A once-exquisite dining table lies in pieces. A family of dead bodies, buzzing with flies, lies face-down in their last meal. Suddenly, one corpse stirs and raises its decaying gaze toward you — you recognize her as a relative from your past! "Mother? Is that you?" she convulses. Her jaw dislocates and black shadow spills from her mouth like a river of lost souls. She falls back, inanimate. A powerful wispy shadow now floats before you — and more shadows spill from the other corpses. Roll initiative!
GM Notes Spawn half as many Shadow-Touched wraiths as there are PCs (round down; use just 1 for a shorter fight). See Shadow-Touched Wraith in the Bestiary. Nothing valuable here besides a couple of rotting doors on the west wall leading onward.

#5 The Baby Doll Sisters Twins' Bedroom

Read on entry Around two skeletal bed frames and loose rubble sit two creepy baby dolls. The one near the western door is covered in fresh blood with an X on its forehead. Suddenly the dolls animate, rising as if guided by an underworldly force. The friendlier one smiles and giggles, "I killed my sister!"
GM Notes Ela and Lila's spirits attempt to possess the nearest PCs, forcing them to attack allies. A possessed PC's spirit-doll returns to its body if the possessed PC drops to 0 HP. If the dolls are destroyed, the girls' ghosts become friendly, disembodied, and follow the party — begging them to break the curse. No treasure here.
Teen table note This is the module's biggest "jump scare" beat — lean into the theatrical creep factor (singsong voice, sudden violence) rather than dwelling on gore.

#6 Sister, Bloody, Sister Bell's Bedroom

Read on entry This once-fine bedchamber is now a scene of massacre. Dried blood and gore cover everything; an unrecognizable, mutilated body appears to have been mauled in its sleep by some sort of beast.
GM Notes The body is Bell, the eldest Reaper daughter. Old ladies' linens and a dagger sit in the drawers. A diary is hidden under the mattress (DC 16 Perception). Ten minutes of reading reveals Bell noticed her father acting strangely — washing blood from his hands, hiding bodies in the basement, chains rattling at night. An Investigation check DC 15 confirms the killer wasn't natural — supernatural forces were involved.

#7 Paradise Lost Master Bedroom

Read on entry The master bedchamber is gruesome — two shredded husks of dead bodies rot in the large central bed. The furnishings look tornado-struck.
GM Notes A DC 18 Perception/Investigation check finds a loose floorboard hiding a second diary and a coin purse with 100 gp. This diary recounts the backstory in The Curse Unleashed (omitting the massacre). Ellemon wanted to destroy the pendant but couldn't bring himself to — it was "precious" to him. The final pages repeat, in chaotic scrawl: "Vecna Forever!" (a clue to the passphrase).

#8 The Ritual Chamber Basement — Finale

Passphrase for both arcane locks: "Vecna Forever"  |  Dispel Magic: DC 22

Read on descent A ladder built into the wall descends into magical darkness. Rattling chains and horrific ritual chanting assault your senses. After climbing down for some time, you reach a landing atop a set of stairs, a soft red glow in the distance. That's when you hear a maiden crying "Help! Help!" from below. A closed wrought-iron gate bars the stairwell — it has an arcane lock.
Read at the base of the stairs Another black iron gate blocks a large chamber with red glowing magic circles on the floor. Cultists and chain-wielding devils contain a young wizardess within one of the circles with ritual chanting and dancing chains.
The Fight 3 Cultists of Vecna (one wears the Pendant of Vecna Influence), 2 Chain Devils, 1 Shadow-Touched Chain Devil, and 1 Shadow-Touched Horned Devil. If the enemies fail a DC 16 group Perception check, the party gets a surprise round. The cultists fight to the death. If the fight is too easy, add 1–2 Shadow-Touched Ghosts that try to possess nearby PCs. The last surviving fiend teleports away rather than dying.
The "Wizardess" Reveal Once freed, she cries into the nearest PC's arms — that PC must save against her charm as she reveals herself as a Vecna-Touched Siren of Shadows (succubus) and attacks. Defacing or destroying her magic circle instead banishes her outright, no fight required — a good option if you're short on time or want to avoid the charm mechanic entirely.
Loot Cultist corpses carry curved cultist daggers and the Pendant of Vecna Influence. The walls hold dark arcana tomes and handwritten ledgers of extracted secrets. The altar bears Vecna's symbol (Religion DC 15 to identify as Vecna, Lord of Dark Secrets, if not already known).
Breaking the Curse An Arcana check DC 18 on the identified pendant reveals that destroying the Pendant of Vecna Influence (dissolve in holy water for 1 hour) is the only way to free Ela and Lila's spirits and lift the family curse. Once freed, the sisters thank the party in an emotional moment before flying away with angel wings and glowing halos.
Teen table note The "defacing the magic circle" option is a great built-in escape hatch if you want to skip the succubus charm/kiss mechanic entirely — a clever or cautious table can end this fight without ever triggering it.

9 Magic Item

Pendant of Vecna Influence

Wondrous item, very rare (requires attunement)

A gold chain holds a small, grim pendant shaped like a severed left hand clutching a single desiccated eye. It radiates a cold, watching malice.

Curse

Any creature that touches the pendant must succeed on a DC 16 Wisdom saving throw or become cursed. A cursed creature is compelled to attune to the pendant at the first opportunity (Remove Curse or similar magic ends the compulsion, but not the attunement once it happens). A cursed, attuned wearer grows obsessed with dark secrets and becomes a willing servant of Vecna — deceiving, harming, or even killing others to learn or protect secrets.

Warlock Boons (while attuned)

You gain the Eldritch Blast cantrip. You also gain one 3rd-level spell slot usable only to cast dispel magic, counterspell, or fear; it recharges after a long rest.

Vecna's Whisper

Once attuned, Vecna himself can speak telepathically to the wearer — and, at the DM's discretion, issue commands the cursed wearer feels driven to obey.

Destroying It

The pendant cannot be destroyed by ordinary means. It can only be destroyed by fully submerging it in holy water for one hour.

GM Tip If nobody in the party has holy water, a temple, shrine, or good-aligned NPC (Eldon!) can bless a vial of water on the spot — don't let this become a dead end.
10 Ending the Session

If the Pendant Is Destroyed

Read aloud A brilliant light pours from the dissolving pendant as its curse finally breaks. From somewhere in the ruined house, you hear two small voices laugh — really laugh, for the first time in a hundred years. Ela and Lila appear before you, no longer trapped in porcelain, but as glowing, translucent spirits with small feathered wings and faint halos. "Thank you," they say together, "you set us free." Their forms brighten, and they rise gently through the ceiling, ascending toward a peace they've waited a century for.

Final Soliloquy (Optional Campaign Hook)

Only read this if you're continuing into a larger campaign (e.g. Vecna: Eve of Ruin). For a clean one-shot ending at a con or club table, you can stop after the twins ascend and skip straight to the debrief below.

Read aloud (optional) As the ruined house grows quiet, Eldon looks out toward the fog-choked skyline of Evernight. "We're likely the only living souls in this whole dark mirror of a city," he says. "If we're getting home, I'd wager there's answers to the east, near the old marketplaces past the cemetery gates." He hesitates. "Fair warning — I've heard merchants here sometimes trade in... parts. Don't ask where from." He forces a tired smile. "A 'crevice of dusk' is what the old texts call a rift home. Let's go find one."
GM Notes This closing beat is a hook into the wider "Vecna: Eve of Ruin" storyline the module was originally written to feed into. It's entirely optional flavor — for a self-contained one-shot, simply congratulate the party on breaking the curse and move to the debrief.
